# Rights note: insurance claim timelines

Background for filing and status calls, in general terms; not legal advice.
Verify for the user's state before citing any number.

## Say out loud

"Could you tell me when the claim will be acknowledged and when a decision is due, so the customer can track both?"
Ask it once on a filing or status call, and put both dates in the read-back.

## The general rule

Most states have an unfair claims settlement practices law, many based on the NAIC model act.
That model makes it an unfair practice, when flagrant or a general business practice, to fail to acknowledge claim communications with reasonable promptness or to affirm or deny coverage within a reasonable time after investigating.
Many states also set day counts; the NAIC model regulation uses 15 days to acknowledge a claim and 21 days after proof of loss to accept or deny it.
The counts vary by state and by line of insurance, so verify for the user's state.

## California example

California's Fair Claims Settlement Practices Regulations (California Code of Regulations, title 10, section 2695.1 and following) set these outer limits:

- Within 15 calendar days of a notice of claim, the insurer must acknowledge it, send the needed forms and instructions, and begin any necessary investigation (section 2695.5(e)).
- Within 40 calendar days of receiving proof of claim, it must accept or deny the claim, in whole or in part (section 2695.7(b)).
- If it needs more time, it must say so in writing within those 40 days and again every 30 calendar days (section 2695.7(c)(1)).
- Once it accepts a claim, it generally must pay within 30 calendar days (section 2695.7(h)).

Proof of claim means documentation that reasonably supports the amount of the loss (section 2695.2(s)), so the 40 days can start well after the first call.

## How the agent uses it

File and check status only; the customer confirms any adjuster visit directly.
California's public adjuster license covers anyone who, for compensation, "aids in any manner" an insured in negotiating or effecting a property claim settlement (Insurance Code section 15007, as cited by the Department of Insurance).
So the agent never negotiates, and whether paid filing help is covered needs legal review.
